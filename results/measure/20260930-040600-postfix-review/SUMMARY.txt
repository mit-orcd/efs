Mechanism, from the code:

1. `host_sender` (raft_host.c ~690–800) sends up to `depth` frames and then
   blocks in `efs_conn_recv_msg` for each `RAFT_REPLY`. For a TCP peer conn
   it sets `HOST_SEND_IO_MS` = 250 ms (the comment at raft_host.c:45–49
   says why: "a dead peer must not sit on the 30 s pool SO_RCVTIMEO or its
   outbox becomes a 30 s/message drip"). For an RDMA peer conn that branch
   is skipped (`if (pc->kind == EFS_CONN_TCP …)`) and the wait is
   `conn_rdma_frame` → `efs_rdma_recv_wait(c->rc, EFS_IO_TIMEOUT_MS)` =
   **30 000 ms** (protocol.c:380, common.h:64). A peer frame that is not
   answered holds the whole lane — heartbeats included — for 30 s, then
   `fail++` and the conn is rebuilt. That is exactly the `sent`-frozen /
   `fail+1` pairs above, and 30 s is 60 election timeouts.
2. During that hold the peer hears no heartbeat, campaigns (`election_ticks`
   100 × 5 ms), and its VOTE_REQ (which does get through — votes replace a
   queued heartbeat in `host_send` and the other lanes are alive) carries a
   higher term. `on_vote_req` → `maybe_step_down` steps the live leader
   down unconditionally (raft.c:1051); there is no Pre-Vote and no
   "heard from the leader within the election timeout → refuse" check. The
   leader re-wins 0.6 s later, the stalled peer campaigns again, repeat.
   fcstor006 at 04:04:56–04:06:38Z is the textbook disruptive server: a
   behind, half-connected node that inflates the term 50× and takes the
   leader down each time.
3. Client cost of each cycle: every group-2 read waits `HOST_READ_TRIES`
   400 ms → BUSY (`wait_timeouts`: fcstor004 0→1290 in the ecopy window,
   fcstor005 57), the client retries 16× (10.3 s) and returns EBUSY. fuse
   log for pid 378525 (client-log-and-ecopy.txt): 21 `shard=0 type=43`
   (LOOKUP) exhausted, 8 `dual type=67` (REPORT) exhausted, ~20 more
   LOOKUP/SETATTR shards exhausted, and a stream of `retry type=43
   why=no-hint from=2/3` (hintless NOT_PRIMARY from fcstor005/006 during
   the elections — legitimate this time; `addr2line`: LOOKUP
   handler.c:630, UNLINK :662, REPORT :839). The 55–59 s `newfstatat`
   walls in ecopy are LOOKUP → 16 × 400 ms + backoff, several times per
   path component. The 96 727-record REPORT (`report-split nrec=96727
   pack_ms=846 push_ms=217 finish_ms=201 rc=0` on fcstor004) was first
   answered `rc=-15` NOT_PRIMARY four times by fcstor005.

What is not known yet: where the RAFT_REPLY goes for 30 s. The frames of
the blocked batch DO reach the peer and are processed: fcstor006 won
elections (04:04:55, 04:05:26, …) while both of its lanes showed `sent`
frozen, so its VOTE_REQ was in a batch whose sends completed and whose
replies never came. `st_sent` counts acked frames only, which is why it
freezes. The receiver's handler is non-blocking (`server_raft_host_inbox`
copies into a 256-slot inbox and returns BUSY when full — silently
dropping the frame; `server_handle_conn` still sends RAFT_REPLY; drops
are not counted). `pend_push` ring overflow marks the conn broken and
wakes the waiter at once, so it is not a silent 30 s either. What is left:
the peer's `efs_conn_send_msg(RAFT_REPLY)` on its conn thread (a send
that waits for a credit or a send-CQ completion), or the sender node's
shared `recv_poller` not harvesting that reply's completion (one poller
per device, `g_reg_lock` per WC, shared with ~400 client conns under
ecopy). A stack sample of the four sender threads and of the peer-conn
handler threads on the other node during a storm (`eu-stack -p`, or
`perf record -e sched:sched_switch -g`) decides it; the server `--perf`
recorders now running are on-CPU only and will not.

Fixes
-----

Rolled (03:56Z, in tree, uncommitted): the group-0 wedge — `on_ae_req`
replies `match = prev_index + nentries`; `send_idx_cover` in
`become_leader`, `maybe_append_cold`, `efs_raft_change`. Verified by this
review: `ls`/`find`/`du`/rsync above, group 0 commit==applied on all
voters, zero `apply-sleep` before the ecopy.

Mechanical, not yet in tree (the decision already exists in the
raft_host.c:45–49 comment; it was only applied to TCP):
  F1. Bound the RDMA peer lane's reply wait to `HOST_SEND_IO_MS` like the
      TCP lane: give `efs_conn` a per-conn recv timeout that
      `conn_rdma_frame` passes to `efs_rdma_recv_wait` instead of
      `EFS_IO_TIMEOUT_MS`, and have `host_sender` set it on every peer
      conn. Gate: `sent` never frozen across a 5 s `raft-obs` sample while
      `enq` grows; no `fail` step of exactly one after a 30 s gap.
  F2. Count inbox drops: `server_raft_host_inbox` returning BUSY is a lost
      Raft message today with no line and no counter; print it on the
      `raft-obs` line (`inbox_drop=`) and log the first ten.

Design, ask before writing (Raft §4.2.3 / §9.6, not in the spec):
  D15. Leader stickiness: a voter that received an AppendEntries from the
       current leader within the last election timeout does not grant a
       vote and does not step down on a higher-term VOTE_REQ (Pre-Vote or
       CheckQuorum are the two standard shapes). Without it, F1 shortens
       each disruption from 30 s to 250 ms but a stalled peer still takes
       the leader down once per stall.
  D16. Whether peer (Raft) frames should share the RDMA device's single
       shared recv CQ / poller and the per-conn credit scheme with client
       traffic at all, or get their own QP class with reserved credits.
       Depends on the stack sample above; do not pick before it.
