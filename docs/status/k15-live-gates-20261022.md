- Normal build: `make -j8 EFS_GIT_ID=163241584ead EFS_GIT_BRANCH=devel-k15gates efsd efs-fuse efs-mgmt`.
  The binaries report `build=163241584ead`. sha256 prefixes: efsd `8bca9bde06914d0b`,
  efs-fuse `5e2c08fb551065e5`, efs-mgmt `06c2f55abc8c00d9`.
- Fault build, in a second copy of the tree:
  `make -j8 EFS_FAULTS=1 EFS_GIT_ID=163241584ead-faults ...` (same targets).

## Environment

- Host intel2: Intel Core Ultra 7 256V, 8 CPUs, 15 GiB RAM, Rocky Linux 10.2,
  kernel 6.12.0-211.64.1.el10_2.
- Stores on XFS `/home` (LVM), buffered I/O (the harness default
  `--no-direct-io`), quota 8G, TCP on 127.0.0.1.
- The host also runs the user's live EFS cluster (efsd on ports 17432–17433,
  FUSE mount `/data1/efs/mnt`). Its load during the runs was not measured.
- The two harness lanes ran **concurrently** on separate private clusters:
  - lane A, normal build, PORT 18800 (18801–18803);
  - lane B, fault build, PORT 18850 (18851–18853).
- Each lane is a three-daemon cluster with `EFS_MD_RAFT_N=3` (set by the
  harness), one writer mount and one peer mount. Roots were under
  `~/efs-k15gates/run/<label>`.

The parameter values are the ones the earlier evidence page suggests.

Stopping: SIGINT to the harness main PID. The harness then unmounts and stops
its daemons. Its writer processes stayed blocked on the mounts, and three
mounts were left behind; `fusermount3 -u` removed them. The queued runs were
skipped by giving them non-empty roots, which the harness refuses.

## Results

### Smoke (normal build, 03:42Z)

PASS. 400 records, 4 writers, wall 11.4 s. Warm, peer and cold views all
show 400 acknowledged records exactly once, with no holes. Final fsync max
0.39 s. 0 REPORT retries. Write latency p50 0.10 s, max 0.38 s.

### Common symptom: both clusters stalled at the same time

Lanes A and B started at 03:43:04Z. Both went from about 10 records/s to
near zero at about 03:46Z, recovered only at about 03:57Z, and were still
slow when they were stopped.

| Elapsed | lane A visible blocks | lane B visible blocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 30 s | 627 | 619 |
| 120–140 s | 1,388 | 1,477 |
| 210–230 s | 1,668 | 1,643 |
| 846–874 s | 1,709 | 1,785 |
| 904–936 s | 2,120 | 1,930 (done) |

Averaged over each run, that is about 2.2 records/s per lane. The average
hides a stall of about 10 minutes.

During the stall, each lane showed:

- client REPORT (67) and APPEND_RESERVE (71) round trips of 1–10 s
  (`slow-ok` p50 3–4 s, max 7.4 s), plus 30 s receive timeouts;
- server `raft-host: report resolve ... rc=-13 (retry)` (BUSY);
- `txn-recover ... decide-abort rc=-13` on coordinated transactions aged
  10–17 s. These happened **without any leader change**: lost-1 had no
  failover events.

The leader's Raft log grew fast:

| Lane | group 0 log index | records | entries per record | leader `apply-sleep` lines | `apply-sleep` p50 | `apply-sleep` max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 120,867 | 2,120 | about 57 | 2,613 | 77 ms | 7.0 s |
| B | 100,072 | 1,971 | — | 2,318 | 88 ms | 6.8 s |

In both lanes, followers rejected AppendEntries (`prev-beyond-log`) through
the whole run. The cause is not established: it could be load from the host
(the two lanes plus the user's cluster), a software stall, or both. Host
iowait was not recorded.

### W52 20k, run w52-1 (lane A): FAIL indication, interrupted

- Interrupted at 936 s, with 2,120 of 20,000 records visible.
- The client log has **26 `inode-rpc: retry type=67 ... why=recv recv_ms=30000`**
  lines. The first was at 03:46:39Z, `req_len` was 852–1532 and attempts reached 6.
- The gate requires zero such retries. So this run would have failed the
  REPORT check whatever its later outcome.
- Final fsync, exact-once and digest checks were not reached.
- REPORT round trips that succeeded but were slow-logged: n=22, p50 4.4 s,
  max 7.4 s.
- No failover events (W52 runs without failover).

### W64 lost reply, run lost-1 (lane B): FAIL

Harness summary: 2,000 records (4 writers on the primary mount, 1 peer
writer), wall 916 s.

| Check | Result | Expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| acknowledged | 1,971 | — |
| failed writes | 29 (15 EIO, 14 EBUSY) | 4 |
| lost replies injected | 4 | ≥ 1 |
| `abandons_ok` | 13 | 4 |
| `abandons_failed` | 2 | 0 |
| `retired_by_abandon` | 5 | — |
| holes (warm, peer and cold) | 5 | 4 |
| REPORT receive timeouts | 12 | 0 |
| server report-resolve retries | 36 | — |
| final fsync max | 0.81 s | rc 0 for every writer |
| peer writer | 400/400 acked, 80 s, final fsync 0.14 s | — |

- **Data integrity held in every view.** Warm primary, warm peer and cold
  all show 1,971 acknowledged records exactly once. There are 0 duplicates,
  0 missing, 0 unacknowledged visible, 0 unknown, 0 order errors and no
  torn or foreign blocks. The size is 4 KiB × 1,976 and the sha256 matches
  in all three views.
- **The four injected losses behaved as designed.** Each one, at seq
  101/198/295/392 between 03:43:11Z and 03:43:35Z, was followed within 15 ms
  by `append-abandon ... rc=0 retired=1`.
- **The verdict fails because of the stall above, not because of the
  injected losses.** The harness assumes every failed write is an injected
  loss. Under the stall, real timeouts produced more failures:
  - At 03:46:41Z an APPEND_RESERVE timed out (`retry type=71 ... why=recv`).
    The client abandoned that identity (`seq=1245 rc=0 retired=1`), which
    made a fifth zero hole. This is consistent with the lost-reply rule: the
    hole count equals the retiring abandons (5).
  - 9 later abandons (seq 1259–1277) returned `rc=0 retired=0`.
  - 2 abandons returned `rc=-13` (BUSY) with `retired=0` (seq 1271, 1273).
    The client then released those identities without retiring them. No
    stranded frontier followed: the final fsync passed and the size
    matched. The harness counts the BUSY abandons as `abandon_failed`.
  - The 14 EBUSY writes match the W64 symptom from before the fix: append
    transactions held by PREPAREs, which `txn-recover` aborted at 10–17 s.
    Here there was no leader change.
- **T1:** not implicated. No session was fenced in this run, so the T1
  path (abandon capturing a fenced session row) was not exercised.

Symptom for a finding: under sustained load on a three-daemon cluster, with
no leader change, append reserve/REPORT round trips reach 30 s. Coordinated
append transactions are aborted by recovery after 10–17 s, and writes fail
with EBUSY or EIO. Some client abandons return BUSY. Integrity holds, but
the W64 availability criterion (no write fails except injected losses) does
not. The harness also needs a rule for abandons caused by real timeouts:
today it counts every non-injected failure, and the hole it leaves, as a
mismatch.

### W64 lost reply + step-down, run lostsd-1 (lane B): partial, NOTRUN

- Ran 70 s and was then interrupted. There were 8 step-down events, all
  `rc=0`.
- Group 0 leader changed 1→3, 3→2 and 2→1. Group 2 changed once (1→3);
  the other events re-elected the same node.
- One injected loss (seq 215, 03:59:21Z) was abandoned 75 ms later with
  `rc=0 retired=1` after several leader changes.
- No verdict: the run did not finish.

## Limitations and NOTRUN

- The 20k W52 gate, both failover modes, both lost-reply modes and every
  second run are NOTRUN. The W52 A/B is NOTRUN (no A/B in sub-sessions).
- Both lanes ran concurrently on a host shared with the user's live
  cluster. The common stall may be partly host load, but the harness checks
  have no load exemption. Rerun on a dedicated host, one lane at a time,
  with host iowait sampled:
  - at the pre-stall 10 records/s, about 35 minutes per 20k run;
  - at the observed average of 2.2 records/s, about 2.5 hours per 20k run.
- Raw logs and `report.json` files stayed on intel2 under
  `~/efs-k15gates/run` and were deleted at the end. Every figure above comes
  from the harness's JSON summaries or from counted log lines. Reproducing
  needs only the commands above.

Cleanup: every harness, daemon and mount from this run was stopped. intel2
was handed back at about 04:01Z. `~/efs-k15gates` was deleted. Nothing of
the user's cluster was touched.
