## ior hard EIO (W161)

At 14:18 one rank's `write()` returned EIO at offset 554,694,400 and ior
aborted on its `rc >= 0` assertion. The node9901 client log for the shared
file (inode 4642):

- 14:17:36 n1: `report-split … stale=1 … rc=-14` (STALE) for one REPORT.
- 14:17:42 client: `report-resolve … rc=-13 … outcome not known; same-identity
  lookup`, then `outcome=partial rc=-14 first_rc=-13`;
  `background-pressure ino=4642 rc=-14 retained`.
- 14:17:50–14:18:33: the retained error repeats; `fsync` and `flush-meta`
  return stale generation (-14) and once BUSY (-13), until the file is
  removed. The cluster stayed OK (3 up) and later runs were clean.

One failure in four runs; not reproduced in three reruns.

### Investigation (Oct 25, from the retained logs and devel source)

**One chunk with a stale span base livelocked the REPORT.** n1 logged the
same rejection 30+ times between 14:17:34 and 14:17:38:
`pub-stale why=span-base ino=4642 ci=2033 rec_base=c28987f070b5f01a
span=65984+47008 want=c28987f070b5f01a have=244b546dfa67ace7`. The client
sent a span record for chunk 2033 that was based on generation `c289…`, while
the committed generation was `244b…`. It sent the same base every round, so
the STALE replay never rebased that chunk.

- **Client side:** at 14:17:36 a REPORT of 787 chunks committed 571. The
  other 216 then replayed every ~100 ms with `committed=0 replayed=216`
  until the 8 s budget ran out. Then `report-resolve` found the receipt
  `partial`, mapped it to STALE (`inode_rpc.c`
  `efs_client_rpc_report_resolve`), and the error stayed with the inode.
  Every later fsync and flush returned it.
- **Why one chunk blocks 216:** the server publishes a REPORT's units as
  one transaction (K32), and a STALE record loses its whole unit
  (`host_pub_units_publish … lost`).
- **Why the base never moves (hypothesis, not yet proven):** a span
  record's CAS base comes from the putid entry (`putid_fill`:
  `rec->base_gen = p->span_base_gen`, fixed when that span was PUT). The
  STALE replay (`dcache_replay_stale_ex_budgeted`, `write.c`) returns
  `EFS_OK` and does nothing when the chunk's dcache entry has no body
  (`if (!e || !e->data || !e->len) return EFS_OK;`). It neither re-PUTs
  the span on the new base nor drops the putid entry, so the next round
  rebuilds the identical record. The body can be gone: the client was
  under dcache pressure (`dcache-pressure … failed=body` at 14:17:16–50),
  and the commit path frees a span body once its own object commits
  (`owns_body`, around `write.c:7690`). With 47,008-byte transfers, two
  ranks' spans share chunk 2033. One span committing (`244b…`) can free
  the body while the other span's putid still names base `c289…`.
- **Why it is intermittent:** it needs two spans of one chunk in flight from
  one client, one committing between the other's PUT and its REPORT, and
  the body released before the replay. That depends on timing and on cache
  pressure.
- **Next step to prove it:** repeat ior hard with `dcache` tracing on the
  node9901 mount until a failure. Then confirm that the replay hit the
  body-less early return for the stuck chunk. A fix would make that replay
  fetch the committed image and rebuild the span on it, or fail closed
  with a distinct error, instead of returning OK.
