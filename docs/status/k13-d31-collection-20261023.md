## The safety rule

An abandoned ticket's fragment body may be deleted on a member only when
**all four** of these hold at that moment:

1. **The ticket can never take effect.** It is retired REJECTED and its
   orphan record exists. Retirement put its sequence under the stream's
   replay floor. ADMIT, PUBLISH (REPORT or typed) and SETTLE of that ticket
   answer STALE. Its object name derives from the ticket, so no other
   ticket names it. A PUBLISHED ticket never gets an orphan record.
2. **Global revocation is complete.** The session group, read fresh (a
   ReadIndex), shows the client's session ACTIVE at an epoch above the
   ticket's epoch. This means the BEGIN → storage seal → FENCE_LOC → FINISH
   barrier for that epoch has finished. The coordinator checks this before
   each record.
3. **The lane shard refuses the epoch.** The shard's durable rejection floor
   (`reject_below`) is above the ticket's epoch. The member's authority read
   checks it, and so does the apply of every acknowledgement.
4. **The member cannot receive the body again.** The member's own durable
   storage session certificate has a floor above the ticket's epoch. The
   member checks this itself, before asking the group. The seal waits for
   every guarded PUT in progress, so no PUT of that epoch lands after the
   delete. The stored record must also name this exact object and carry the
   ticket's checksum. A different body under that name is never deleted.

A member that is down, unreachable or refusing keeps the record pending.
The collection waits for that member and retries on later passes, without
end. It never declares the record done from age, absence, a quorum or the
other members' acknowledgements. A member removed from the cluster keeps
its records pending, so collecting them needs an operator.

## What changed

- **Orphan ledger.** The orphan record moved to the group's anchor shard
  (`[anchor][PUT_ORPHAN 53][lane shard][uuid][epoch][seq][ino][gen][ci]`,
  53 bytes), so the leader scans one prefix per group. The value is the
  REJECTED ticket value plus the mask of acknowledged fragments.
  `efs_put_ticket_orphan_ack` sets bits only behind the shard floor. The
  apply that sets the last bit deletes the record. An acknowledgement after
  retirement is an OK no-op. A record that does not derive from its own key
  is refused on decode and counted as a failure.
- **Collector step** (`src/meta/put_orphan.c`, `efs_put_orphan_collect`).
  For one record it does four things in order: revocation proof, shard floor,
  one delete per unacknowledged fragment, and one acknowledgement for those
  that succeeded. The record's mask is the only progress state, so a retry,
  a lost reply, a restart or a new leader resumes from the log. Within one
  pass, a member that failed once is skipped for the rest of that pass.
- **Leader pass** (`host_put_orphan_pass`, `src/server/raft_host.c`). It runs
  in the GC maintenance pass, about once a second, on each group this node
  leads. It handles at most 32 records per pass (`EFS_PUT_ORPHAN_PER_PASS`),
  with a cursor. The cursor and the cycle count reset when leadership is lost.
- **Member delete** `EFS_MSG_PUT_ORPHAN_DELETE` (177/178,
  `src/server/put_orphan.c`). It checks, in order:
  - the fragment is this member's own (`nodes[fi]`);
  - the storage session floor is above the epoch (otherwise BUSY);
  - the group's fresh orphan status (`EFS_MD_REQ_PUT_ORPHAN_STATUS` 87, RPC
    only). NOT_FOUND gives STALE.

  It then runs the checksum-guarded durable `server_delete_fragment_if_sum`.
  A different body gives PROTO; absent counts as deleted. Finally it cleans
  up the chunk directories.
- **Log command** `EFS_MD_CMD_PUT_ORPHAN_ACK` (86, 218 bytes). It is in the
  applied-command table. A malformed entry halts the group, and a refusal is
  a logical verdict.
- **Status.** `efs-mgmt status` prints `Abandoned uploads: pending=N
  (group 0, group 2); collected, fragments_deleted, failures`. Pending is the
  number of records left at the end of the last complete scan by each group's
  leader. It reads `unknown` until both leaders have finished a scan.
  `efs_msg_status_reply` grew these fields; no compatibility is kept.

`tests/test_put_orphan` uses the real ledger, the real session state machine
and the real collector step. It runs on an LSM KV that is reopened between
passes, as a new leader or a restart would find it. Three members are
modelled on in-memory fragment stores, in the member delete's order.
Scenarios:

- **Safety rule.** Before revocation, a pass deletes nothing and counts every
  record as waiting. A direct acknowledgement is BUSY (no shard floor), and a
  member delete is BUSY (no storage floor). After BEGIN, the storage seals
  and FENCE_LOC, nothing is deleted until FINISH completes the global
  barrier.
- **Per-member acknowledgement with a member down.** Fragments on members 1
  and 2 are deleted and acknowledged (mask 3). The record is retained. Five
  further passes and a KV reopen change nothing, and the body on member 3
  stays.
- **Lost acknowledgement.**
  - Lost before commit: the fragment is gone but the mask is unchanged. The
    next pass deletes again (absent counts as OK), acknowledges and retires.
  - Lost after commit: the record is already retired, and a duplicate
    acknowledgement is an OK no-op.
- **Racing re-admission.** For an abandoned ticket, ADMIT is STALE before
  revocation and FENCED after. PUBLISH is STALE, and a late SETTLE is a
  no-op that keeps the record.
- **Checksum guard.** A different body under an orphan's object name is
  neither deleted nor acknowledged in three passes. The record stays
  pending and each pass counts one failure.
- **Negative control: a published body is never deleted.** The published
  ticket has no orphan record. A direct member delete of any of its fragments
  answers STALE. An acknowledgement for it is a no-op. All three fragments
  and the chunk mapping remain. A live, never-settled ticket is refused in
  the same way.
- **Codec.** The record is on the anchor shard and decodes back to the exact
  ticket. A changed object, a non-REJECTED state, a wrong anchor or a full
  mask is refused.

`tests/test_put_orphan_server` runs the actual member code
(`server_put_orphan_delete` and the durable storage certificate) with the
authority and the disk mocked:

- a malformed or foreign frame is refused before any authority or disk work;
- with no certificate, or a floor equal to the epoch, the delete is BUSY;
- an authority NOT_FOUND (published, live or collected) gives STALE with no
  delete;
- with authority BUSY or NOT_PRIMARY, nothing is deleted;
- a checksum mismatch gives PROTO with no cleanup;
- an I/O error is passed through;
- a successful delete runs on the exact object and checksum, cleans up
  durably and leaves the caller's chunk generation unchanged; a retry is OK.

## Limitations and remaining gates

- **Dead clients' unsettled tickets are not collected yet.** A client that
  dies before SETTLE leaves ADMITTED or REJECTED tickets in its stream. They
  never become orphan records, so their bodies still leak after revocation.
  The rule above covers them. What is missing is a revoked-stream drain: a
  leader command that moves such tickets to the orphan ledger behind the
  shard floor, refuses while a K14 stage is pending, and retires PUBLISHED
  heads. That command also needs an anchored per-stream index, so the leader
  avoids scanning 2048 shards. The older `reclaim_begin`/`delete_ack`/
  `retire` RECLAIMING path in `put_ticket.c` is still unused.
- **Live owners.** A body that a live client abandons waits until an
  operator revokes that session (`efs-mgmt` session fence). A clean unmount
  does not revoke it. Collection therefore depends on revocation; it is not
  automatic.
- **Removed members.** Records whose fragments sit on a removed member stay
  pending, by design.
- **Pending count.** It is the per-cycle count from each group's leader, so
  it can lag by one scan cycle. Unreadable records are counted as pending
  and as failures.
- **Revocation proof per record.** The coordinator reads the session group
  once for each record. Records are not batched by session.
- **NOTRUN:**
  - a private three-daemon gate: abandon, revoke, collect with one member
    stopped, then restart it and leader-kill mid-collection (the parent's
    cluster gate);
  - RDMA, orcd-mini, multi-host, power loss.
