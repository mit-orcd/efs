## Part 1: snapshot to a near follower

**Cause.** A new leader's first commit or read probe is sent with
`prev_index = ae_inflight_end`, which is 0 right after the election. The
follower answers success with `match_index` 0. `on_ae_rep` set
`next_index = match_index + 1 = 1` on every success, which is below the
log floor, so the next send to that follower was an InstallSnapshot. On
orcd-mini, n1 sent n2 a 4.7 GB image (`snap-2-30676475`) although n2 was
12 entries behind. A W147-style `match_index` 0 rejection was not
involved; the zero came from a successful probe.

**Fix.** A success reply only raises `next_index`; only a rejection lowers
it. A prev-term conflict now hints below the follower's whole uncommitted
run of the conflicting term (never below commit or the snapshot point),
so a deposed leader's tail walks back in one round trip instead of one
index per round trip.

**Tests** (`tests/test_raft.c`): `test_probe_ack_keeps_next_index` and
`test_conflict_hint_skips_term_run`. On the old `raft.c` they fail
(8 checks across both fixture modes). With the fix, `test_raft`,
`test_sim` and `test_raft_store` PASS.

## Part 2: was a client acknowledged for 30,929,219@62?

**Answer: no.** No proposer on n2 could have reported success for any of
the stray term-62 entries 30,929,219–30,929,230.

How a proposer decides (source at `7c510c60f`, unchanged since the
incident build `784bcc6ee` in these functions):
- `host_propose_*` waits once in `host_wait_applied`, bounded by
  `HOST_READ_TRIES x HOST_TICK_US` = 400 ms. On timeout it returns
  `EFS_ERR_BUSY`.
- Only after `applied >= idx` does `host_arc_verdict` read the apply
  ring, and it requires the slot's term to equal the proposal's term.
- No caller waits again on the same (index, term) after a timeout; the
  retry loops propose a new entry.

Evidence (read-only, node9901 `/data1/efs/logs/efsd-n{1,2}.log`,
`/data1/efs/efs-fuse-mnt.log`; node9900
`/data1/efs/orcd-mini-client/logs/efs-fuse-orcd-mini-mnt.log`):
- n2 logged 26 `apply-sleep` lines for `idx` 30,929,219–30,929,230 between
  12:54:45.943Z and 12:54:46.986Z. Each ended at or below the 400 ms
  deadline with `applied=30929217` or `30929218`. The waits started at
  about 12:54:45.5Z, after the 12:54:41–44Z perf stall, so n2 appended
  these entries after it resumed, still believing it led term 62.
- n2 applied 30,929,219@62 at 12:54:49Z (W145's raft.log reconstruction),
  more than two seconds after the last of those waits had returned BUSY.
  n2 logged no wait on these indices after 12:54:47Z.
- n1 applied its own 30,929,219–30,929,230 (term 63) at 12:54:46.7–46.9Z.
- node9901's client logged only `inode-rpc: slow-ok ... status=0`
  retries in 12:54–12:55Z (one with `saw_busy=1` at 12:54:46.680Z) and
  no error returns. node9900's client logged no inode RPC in that window.
- n2's raft.log no longer holds the 30,929,219@62 record: the 16:20Z
  `raft-resync` install replaced its group-2 log up to 33,953,093. The
  efsd and fuse logs above still cover the window.

Log hashes at read time (2026-10-22 ~19:55Z): `efsd-n2.log` sha256
`9bbb69fd…`, `efs-fuse-mnt.log` sha256 `a383ae7e…`.

Residual exposure: between 12:54:49Z and the 16:20Z repair, n2's state
machine held the stray entry. A read served by n2 needed n2's applied

## Part 3: hundreds of elections per restart

**Cause.** Each efsd starts its Raft groups before its peers listen (and
before its own accept loop runs), and every election timeout (500 ms to
1 s) raised the term by one. orcd-mini's 16:15Z restart shows it: n1
listened at 16:15:12Z and became group-2 leader at term 1046 (from 923)
when n2 arrived at 16:15:23Z; n3 had campaigned alone to term 1120
before it listened at 16:15:29Z and deposed n1 on contact. Only one or
two leaders were elected per restart; the hundreds of terms were lone
candidacies. Startup inventory time widens the window.

**Fix.** Pre-vote (Raft thesis 9.6). A follower whose timer fires asks
at term+1 without changing or persisting its term; a quorum of grants
starts the real election. A voter grants only if it has not heard from a
leader within the minimum election timeout and the candidate's log is at
least as up to date. New wire types `EFS_RAFT_MSG_PREVOTE_REQ/REP` (no
mixed builds). The vote, became-leader, step-down and prevote lines are
rate limited per group like `ae_reject_note` (first, then one per 10 s,
with event and skipped counts).

**Tests.** `test_restart_elections_bounded` and
`test_prevote_does_not_disturb` in `tests/test_raft.c` fail on the old
`raft.c` (18 checks) and pass.

**Cluster reproduction** (`scripts/k01_restart_elections.sh`): private
3-daemon cluster on efs1 (Fedora 42 aarch64, TCP, buffered I/O, quota
2G, `EFS_MD_RAFT_N=3`, ports 18101-18103, stores under `/data1/k01`).
It formats, stops, restarts n1, n2 and n3 15 s apart, then reads each
group's term with `efs-mgmt raft-status`.

## Part 4: `stop.sh` with a blocked handler

**Cause.** orcd-mini n2's shutdowns logged `shutdown: waiting for N live
handlers` for 80-180 s (14:19Z, 15:22Z, 15:32Z, 15:50Z, 16:12Z). During
the 15:22Z one, n2 logged a 400 ms group-0 `read-sleep` (`pending=1`)
about every 1.4 s: a handler serving a pooled client's retried read,
which could not get a quorum because the peers were already stopping.
Shutdown wakes idle handlers with TCP `SHUT_RD`, but an RDMA-upgraded
connection's requests arrive on the RDMA channel, which
`efs_conn_wait_request` checks first, so the handler kept serving
retries. stop.sh's `server_processes.py stop-file` waits 60 s, then gives
up without SIGKILL.

**Fix.** When the accept loop exits, `server_handlers_begin_stop` makes
every handler close its connection before reading its next request.
`shutdown_drain` gives handlers 40 s (a group wait is 400 ms, a forwarded
mutation's RPC 30 s). A handler still live then is refused with a log
line, and efsd exits without teardown (rc 3) rather than freeing state
under it or hanging. The Raft log is fsynced before every
acknowledgement, so an untorn exit is a crash restart.

**Test.** `tests/test_shutdown_bounded.py` compiles the real gate and
drain: a handler serving an endless retry stream is released about 50 ms
after the stop, and a stuck handler is refused at the deadline. The
private efs1 cluster stopped without hitting the drain deadline.

**Not reproduced.** efs1 has no RDMA, so the RDMA blocked-handler case
itself ran only in the unit test. orcd-mini deploy gate NOTRUN.
