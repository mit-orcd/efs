## Part 1: snapshot to a near follower

**Cause.** A new leader's first commit or read probe is sent with
`prev_index = ae_inflight_end`, which is 0 right after the election. The
follower answers success with `match_index` 0. `on_ae_rep` set
`next_index = match_index + 1 = 1` on every success, which is below the
log floor, so the next send to that follower was an InstallSnapshot. On
orcd-mini, n1 sent n2 a 4.7 GB image (`snap-2-30676475`) although n2 was
12 entries behind. A W147-style `match_index` 0 rejection was not
involved; the zero came from a successful probe.

**Fix.** A success reply only raises `next_index`; only a rejection lowers
it. A prev-term conflict now hints below the follower's whole uncommitted
run of the conflicting term (never below commit or the snapshot point),
so a deposed leader's tail walks back in one round trip instead of one
index per round trip.

**Tests** (`tests/test_raft.c`): `test_probe_ack_keeps_next_index` and
`test_conflict_hint_skips_term_run`. On the old `raft.c` they fail
(8 checks across both fixture modes). With the fix, `test_raft`,
`test_sim` and `test_raft_store` PASS.

## Part 2: was a client acknowledged for 30,929,219@62?

**Answer: no.** No proposer on n2 could have reported success for any of
the stray term-62 entries 30,929,219–30,929,230.

How a proposer decides (source at `7c510c60f`, unchanged since the
incident build `784bcc6ee` in these functions):
- `host_propose_*` waits once in `host_wait_applied`, bounded by
  `HOST_READ_TRIES x HOST_TICK_US` = 400 ms. On timeout it returns
  `EFS_ERR_BUSY`.
- Only after `applied >= idx` does `host_arc_verdict` read the apply
  ring, and it requires the slot's term to equal the proposal's term.
- No caller waits again on the same (index, term) after a timeout; the
  retry loops propose a new entry.

Evidence (read-only, node9901 `/data1/efs/logs/efsd-n{1,2}.log`,
`/data1/efs/efs-fuse-mnt.log`; node9900
`/data1/efs/orcd-mini-client/logs/efs-fuse-orcd-mini-mnt.log`):
- n2 logged 26 `apply-sleep` lines for `idx` 30,929,219–30,929,230 between
  12:54:45.943Z and 12:54:46.986Z. Each ended at or below the 400 ms
  deadline with `applied=30929217` or `30929218`. The waits started at
  about 12:54:45.5Z, after the 12:54:41–44Z perf stall, so n2 appended
  these entries after it resumed, still believing it led term 62.
- n2 applied 30,929,219@62 at 12:54:49Z (W145's raft.log reconstruction),
  more than two seconds after the last of those waits had returned BUSY.
  n2 logged no wait on these indices after 12:54:47Z.
- n1 applied its own 30,929,219–30,929,230 (term 63) at 12:54:46.7–46.9Z.
- node9901's client logged only `inode-rpc: slow-ok ... status=0`
  retries in 12:54–12:55Z (one with `saw_busy=1` at 12:54:46.680Z) and
  no error returns. node9900's client logged no inode RPC in that window.
- n2's raft.log no longer holds the 30,929,219@62 record: the 16:20Z
  `raft-resync` install replaced its group-2 log up to 33,953,093. The
  efsd and fuse logs above still cover the window.

Log hashes at read time (2026-10-22 ~19:55Z): `efsd-n2.log` sha256
`9bbb69fd…`, `efs-fuse-mnt.log` sha256 `a383ae7e…`.

Residual exposure: between 12:54:49Z and the 16:20Z repair, n2's state
