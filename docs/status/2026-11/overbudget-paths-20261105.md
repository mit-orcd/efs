## Findings

1. **HELLO relay wait cycle (K91, product, fixed).** A slow F14 run is 6 s to form 16 members and 26 to 28 s more at the end: node 1's stop logged `shutdown: waiting for 1 live handlers` every 5 s, then `closing connection before the next request` and `elapsed_ms=26665` (every other member stopped in 0.1 to 0.5 s). gdb on node 1 during the wait: the live handler was in `server_handle_conn` -> `server_gossip_membership` -> `gossip_hello_to(node 2)`, blocked in `efs_conn_recv_msg` on a pooled peer connection, deadline about 30 s after the call began (`EFS_IO_TIMEOUT_MS`, the peer-pool receive timeout, `peer_pool.c`). gdb on node 2 at the same moment: its thread for that connection was itself in `gossip_hello_to(node X)`, and `ss` showed node 1's next HELLO unread in node 2's socket (`Recv-Q 4269`). A member that changes its node table on a HELLO sends the ACK and then relays the HELLO serially to every peer on the same connection thread, and every member a relay changed relayed it again; with 13 joiners at once these relays waited on each other in cycles until the 30 s timeout. Fix: the relay copy carries a new `EFS_HELLO_F_RELAY` bit (`protocol.h`, accepted by `efs_peer_hello_decode`) and a receiver applies a relayed HELLO without relaying it. The first receiver still reaches every peer it knows, and the committed membership adds members to every table on its own (`membership: member N added` on apply). No wire compatibility kept (project rule).
2. **Read retry sleep against a reaped generation (K92, product, fixed).** In K38 the first full read of the shared file (whichever mount read first) took 4.9 to 5.4 s and the next two 0.6 to 0.9 s; daemons and client logged nothing in that window and server GETs averaged under 0.4 ms, with `errors=351` then `991` on node 1. gdb samples of that `efs-fuse`: read-pool workers in `read_retry_pause(delay_us=100000)` from `efs_client_decode_placed_chunk_attempts` (`read.c`), the others queued behind them in `rdcache_acquire`. A fold or CAS supersedes a chunk's map and the reaper deletes the old objects; a fetch against the older map gets NOT_FOUND on every fragment, which no retry of the same map can fix, but the decode slept 100 ms and retried before `efs_client_fetch_published_chunk_obs` pulled the current row. Fix: when every failed fragment answered NOT_FOUND the decode returns at once (rc unchanged, `EFS_ERR_DECODE`) for the row pull; network and BUSY failures keep the backoff.
3. **RDMA setup under a spent read-proof budget (K94, product, not fixed).** The remaining slow K38 runs spend 18 to 22 s in the write phase. Their daemons log 62 to 269 `segmented RDMA setup failed or incompatible peer` and 1 to 8 `RDMA send type=95 FAILED (segmented send failed) ... posted=0` (type 95 is the forwarded submit, `host_remote_cmd`); the fast runs log none. A one-line trace at both failure points (scratch tree on node9900, not committed) printed `remaining=0` at all 163 setup and 4 send failures in two slow runs: the calling thread's network deadline had already passed. Read-only forwarded proofs get one 400 ms budget (`HOST_READ_TRIES` x `HOST_TICK_US`) for pool admission, connection, leader retries and frames; `peer_conn_get` checks the budget only before `peer_connect`, which then connects, declares and upgrades under it. A spent budget makes the upgrade's TCP exchange fail without reading a reply (the connection stays TCP) or the first send on a new RDMA connection fail before posting, and `host_remote_cmd_impl` drops that connection as broken; the next proof connects again. Over TCP, 1 of 6 runs showed a milder 14.9 s write phase. Which policy is right (a pooled connection's setup bounded by its own budget, not one caller's leftover; or a pre-post deadline failure that leaves the connection healthy) is a transport decision this task did not make: the RDMA live gates are NOTRUN on these hosts.
4. **Member-store identity race (K93, product, not fixed).** TSan on F14 (both trees): `ms_persist` (`member_store.c`) copies the whole identity (`*ms = *next`) under the Raft host mutex while the rejoin thread reads `s->mstore.token` in `server_join_cluster` (`cluster.c`) under `s->lock`. F14 was not on the TSan list before.

## Limits

- K91's before rate is a few in a hundred on RDMA (2 of about 35 recorded gates, 4 of 27 solo runs); the TCP repro (4 of 7) carries the before/after comparison.
- K94's rate varies by hour on the same host: base 2 of 6, fixed 4 of 6 on idle node9900, on fstor007 1 traced of 8 (one more at 21.1 s, not traced). The base runs show the fix did not cause it; too few runs to say whether the faster read path changes its odds.
- The K94 mechanism is shown by `remaining=0` at every failure; why the proofs arrive at `peer_connect` with the budget spent (leader retries, pool waits, setup cost) is not split further.
- The read fix assumes NOT_FOUND from every failing fragment means a superseded map. If a fragment were NOT_FOUND for another reason, the caller's row pull and its second fetch still run; only the extra same-map attempt after 100 ms is gone.
- The relay fix assumes the first receiver's table is complete enough; members it does not know learn the joiner from the committed membership, which F14 checks on all 16.
- The K54 and B6 failures above were not traced in this task.
