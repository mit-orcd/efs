## The rule

A drain moves a stream into the orphan ledger; it never deletes a body. A
body is still deleted only under the four conditions of the
[collection rule](k13-d31-collection-20261023.md#the-safety-rule). The drain
of one session/lane-shard stream applies only when:

1. **The leader proved revocation.** The session group, read fresh, shows
   the session ACTIVE at an epoch above the stream's (the barrier finished).
   The leader reads this once per session per pass.
2. **The lane shard refuses the epoch.** The apply rechecks this shard's
   durable rejection floor. From then on no ADMIT, PUBLISH, REPORT or SETTLE
   of that epoch applies here, so the stream can no longer change under it.
3. **No K14 stage can still publish the ticket.** A staged multi-chunk
   publication on the ticket's chunk may publish it at RESOLVE, so the drain
   stops at that ticket (BUSY) until the stage is resolved, as the owner's
   SETTLE already does.

Then, from the stream head, in sequence order:

| Head state | Drain action |
| --- | --- |
| PUBLISHED | retire it (the floor advances); no orphan record, the mapping keeps the body |
| REJECTED | retire it into the orphan ledger, as SETTLE does |
| ADMITTED | abandon it (REJECTED) and retire it into the orphan ledger |

## What changed

- **Stream index** (`EFS_KV_KIND_PUT_STREAM` **250**,
  `[anchor:2][250][uuid:16][epoch:4][lane shard:2]`, 25 bytes, value
  `[1:4]`). It lives on the group's anchor shard. The entry is written in the
  batch that takes a stream's live count from 0 to 1, and deleted in the
  batch that takes it from 1 to 0. So the leader scans one prefix per group,
  sorted by session, instead of 2048 lane shards. ADMIT, ADMIT_BATCH, SETTLE,
  SETTLE_BATCH and DRAIN all go through the same two places (`insert_ticket`
  and `retire_head`).
- **Log command** `EFS_MD_CMD_PUT_TICKET_DRAIN` (Raft tag **250**,
  `[tag][uuid:16][epoch:4][lane shard:4]`, 25 bytes,
  `efs_put_ticket_drain`). It retires at most 64 tickets per entry
  (`EFS_PUT_TICKET_DRAIN_MAX`). The verdict is OK when it retired one or the
  stream is empty, and BUSY when nothing could retire. It is in the
  applied-command table. A malformed entry halts, and a refusal is a logical
  verdict (IO/NOMEM retry). A DRAIN applied twice (by an old and a new
  leader) is a no-op the second time.
- **Leader pass** (`host_put_drain_pass`, `efs_put_drain_stream`). It runs in
  the GC maintenance pass of each group this node leads, before the orphan
  collector. It handles 32 streams per pass with a cursor, and at most 8 DRAIN
  commands per stream. The cursor resets when leadership is lost. The index
  and the log are the only progress state.
- **Clean unmount.** `efs_client_shutdown` runs a last settle and then
  `efs_client_session_end`. That function revokes the mount's own session
  with the same I23 barrier the operator uses: BEGIN, storage seal,
  FENCE_LOC and ACK on every touched shard, FINISH, then lease/append
  cleanup. The coordinator moved from `efs-mgmt` into
  `src/common/session_revoke.c` (`efs_session_revoke`), and
  `efs-mgmt raft-session fence` now calls it (same output). If the barrier
  fails, it stays resumable by `efs-mgmt raft-session fence`. The mount logs
  `efs: session revoked at unmount: epoch=… touched=… rc=…`, and
  `EFS_UNMOUNT_REVOKE=0` keeps the session open.
- **RECLAIMING path removed.** `efs_put_ticket_reclaim_begin`,
  `efs_put_ticket_delete_ack` and `efs_put_ticket_retire` were used only by
  tests. They kept per-member deletion state on the stream itself, which
  duplicated the orphan ledger. They are deleted. A stored state 3 (was
  RECLAIMING), or a nonzero acknowledgement word in a ticket row, is now
  refused (PROTO) by every reader, including the client's status probe.
  There is no compatibility path (AGENTS.md: fresh fixtures).

Numbers taken (renumber on collision at merge): KV kind 250, Raft tag 250.
No new wire message type.

`tests/test_put_drain.py` compiles the production leader pass, the DRAIN
apply and K32's ADMIT_BATCH/SETTLE_BATCH apply out of `raft_host.c`. It links
them with the real ticket ledger, session state machine, shared revocation
coordinator and orphan collector. Everything runs on a durable LSM KV that is
reopened as a new leader would find it, with three storage members modelled
as in `test_put_orphan`. The coordinator's transport applies each session
request to the same KV. Scenarios:

1. **Dead client.** Tickets are admitted through ADMIT_BATCH and single
   ADMIT on three streams in both groups, and their bodies are stored. One
   is published and one is rejected; there is no SETTLE.
   - Before revocation: the pass proposes nothing, a direct DRAIN is BUSY,
     and nothing is orphaned or deleted.
   - After revocation: a late SETTLE_BATCH changes nothing, and a late
     ADMIT_BATCH admits nothing (FENCED).
   - The pass then empties all three streams. The published head retires
     without a record, and the five other tickets become orphan records.
   - The collector deletes all five bodies. The published body and its
     mapping stay.
2. **K14 stage.** A real staged publication (`efs_txn_apply_prepare`
   PUBLISH) is pending on an ADMITTED ticket's chunk when the owner is
   revoked. The DRAIN answers BUSY, the ticket stays ADMITTED, and no
   record or deletion happens. After DECIDE and RESOLVE ABORT, the next pass
   drains the stream, and both bodies are reclaimed.
3. **Clean unmount.** The mount's last settle retires what it can: one
   PUBLISHED ticket, one abandoned ticket into the ledger, and a PUBLISHED
   ticket on a second stream. A PUBLISHED ticket stays behind an ambiguous
   ADMITTED one. Then `efs_session_revoke` runs over the transport. After
   the pass and the collector, no ticket row or stream index entry is left
   for the session. The two unpublished bodies are gone, and the three
   published bodies stay.
4. **Leader change.** A 150-ticket stream, after revocation:
   - the first DRAIN proposal is lost before commit;
   - the second commits 64 tickets, but its reply is lost (and a copy of it
     commits again later);
   - leadership is lost at the third proposal.
   The new leader starts with a reopened KV and a reset cursor, and drains
   the remaining 86 in two entries. The old leader's delayed DRAIN then
   applies as a no-op. Every ticket is retired exactly once, there are 150
   orphan records, and all 150 bodies are collected after another reopen.
5. **Wiring (source shape).**
   - The leader drains before it collects, and resets the drain cursor when
     it loses leadership.
   - `efs_client_shutdown` orders the meta flush, the last settle, the
     session end, and then the connection teardown.
   - The session end and `efs-mgmt` both call `efs_session_revoke`.

Four mutants of `efs_put_ticket_drain` and the index were each killed by
this test: no stage check, no floor check, an index entry never removed, and
no orphan record. `tests/test_put_ticket.c` now covers the DRAIN directly
(bounds, a faulted batch, PUBLISHED retirement, abandonment, idempotence,
re-admission FENCED) and a drain after a crash between the shard floor and
FINISH.

## Limitations and remaining gates

- **NOTRUN (parent session):** the live collection gate: a private
  three-daemon cluster, a client killed after ADMIT, then revocation,
  drain and collection, with one member stopped and a leader kill
  mid-drain. Also a clean `fusermount -u` that ends with
  `session revoked at unmount … rc=0` and an empty `Abandoned uploads`
  pending count. RDMA, orcd-mini, multi-host and power loss are NOTRUN too.
- **A stage that never resolves** keeps its ticket's stream BUSY (fail
  closed). The drain refuses on any stage on the chunk, not only one naming
  this ticket, as SETTLE does.
- **A crashed client is still revoked by an operator** (`efs-mgmt
  raft-session fence`). The drain removes the leak after revocation, but
  revoking a dead client automatically (lease expiry) is not part of this
  change.
- **Unmount cost:** the barrier visits every shard the session touched (two
  commits each, plus cleanup). An unreachable cluster fails it quickly with
  NET and leaves the session for an operator.
- **Status:** drain progress shows only as the orphan pending count and, with
  `EFS_GC_DBG=1`, as `put-drain pass` lines; there is no new status field.
