## Dead-code sweep (item 1)

### Method

- **Compiler:** the product binaries were built with gcc 14.3.1
  `-Wall -Wextra` (the Makefile's flags), then again with `-Wunused
  -Wunused-macros` added. The only warning in either build is
  `-Wmissing-field-initializers` in `packer.c:1067`, unrelated to this
  sweep. `-Wunused-function` therefore finds no unused static function, and
  the two unused macros (`EFS_NODES_MAGIC`, `EFS_NODES_VERSION`) are unused
  only in the efs-bench copy of `store.c`.
- **Link time:** `-ffunction-sections -fdata-sections` objects were linked
  with `--gc-sections`, and the globals defined in product objects were
  compared with the symbols left in efsd, efs-fuse, efs-mgmt and efs-bench
  (`scripts/dead_symbols.sh`). At `5a7463440` this lists 186 candidates.
  For each, a grep of `src/`, `tools/` and `tests/` found its callers:
  - 159 are named by unit tests: test oracles such as the in-memory KV,
    store and Raft, and the query catalog builder;
  - 4 are called only by the simulator;
  - 2 are called only from test-only functions in another file:
    `efs_name_index_bytes` from the query catalog oracle, and
    `raft_log_rotate_locked` from `efs_raft_disk_rotate`;
  - 19 have callers only in their own file, and drop out because they are
    inlined. `efs_txid_ns` is also called from `EFS_FAULTS` code;
  - 2 have no caller at all. Both are removed.

  After the removal the list is those two shorter, and nothing new
  appears.
- **Fields:** `scripts/unread_fields.py` lists struct members that are
  never read: no access, only writes, or only `free()`.
- **Targeted search:** handlers, structs, names and comments for every
  removed wire type, flag, variable and mode were checked with grep. This
  covered JOIN, ADD_STORAGE, buffered fragment I/O, `persist_nodes`, the
  balance pump, `query_enabled`, bloom/prefetch/batch switches, NFS export,
  `EFS_EXPIRY` outside `#if EFS_BUILD_EXPIRY`, the RECLAIMING ticket state
  and the `--cluster` admission.
- **Not run:** `cppcheck` and `clang --analyze`. Neither is installed on
  intel2 or amd, and the Mac's clang lacks the Linux and verbs headers.
  This is NOTRUN, not a pass.

## A9 leader placement (item 2)

`scripts/leader_placement.py run --seed ADDR:PORT` measures a live cluster.
It reads the members from `efs-mgmt status`. Every `--interval` it asks
each up member for `efs-mgmt raft-status` in parallel. A group's leader is
the member that reports `role=LEADER` itself; with two claims the higher
term wins, and with none the group counts as leaderless. Each sample holds
until the next one, so shares are of wall time.

Over the run, `--stepdowns` and `--restarts` are spread evenly and
interleaved, taking the groups in turn:
- a step-down asks the group's current leader to stand down with no target
  (`efs-mgmt raft-step-down EP GROUP`), and the next election decides;
- a restart stops that leader, keeps it down `--down` seconds and starts it
  again through `--restart-cmd` (`{node}`, `{ep}`, `{action}`).

It prints the distribution, the leaderless share, leader changes, and the
share of time all groups' leaders sat on one node. `--json` keeps the
samples and events, which `leader_placement.py report RUN.json` prints
again. `tests/test_leader_placement.py` checks it in `make test` on fixed
`raft-status` output: real output captured on intel2, plus synthetic
retired, non-hosted, candidate and competing-term replicas. It also
checks the member table, the time weighting and the event schedule.

`tests/measure/a9_leader_placement_loopback.py` runs it on a private
loopback cluster:
- product `./efsd`, TCP, free loopback ports, stores under `TMPDIR`;
- the cluster forms by itself, then the script runs;
- a restart is SIGTERM, 5 s down, then a start from the same store.

**Run on intel2:** build `1cc38fd97` (the sweep's code, identical to the
gated commit in `src/` and `include/` apart from one comment and one blank line). One
run per size, sequential, nothing else running. Each run lasted 600 s,
sampled every 0.5 s, with 12 step-downs and 6 leader restarts (5 s down):

```bash
TMPDIR=$HOME/a12-tmp python3 tests/measure/a9_leader_placement_loopback.py --members N \
    --duration 600 --interval 0.5 --stepdowns 12 --restarts 6 --down 5 --json placement-mN.json
```

All 12 step-downs and 6 restarts succeeded in every run. Every sample
reached every member that was up (a restarted member is down for its 5 s).
Group 0 votes on nodes {1,2,3}; group 2 on {1,2,3} at three members and on
{2,3,4} from four.

| Members | Group 0 leader share by node | Group 2 leader share by node | Leaderless (g0 / g2) | Leader changes (g0 / g2) | Both leaders on one node |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 3 | 1: 31.6 %, 2: 31.1 %, 3: 36.4 % | 1: 20.9 %, 2: 26.1 %, 3: 52.0 % | 0.9 % / 1.0 % | 10 / 9 | **41.5 %** of the run (42.2 % of the time both were led) |
| 4 | 1: 52.2 %, 3: 47.0 % | 2: 47.0 %, 3: 31.3 %, 4: 20.8 % | 0.8 % / 0.9 % | 8 / 11 | **20.7 %** (21.1 %) |
| 5 | 1: 46.9 %, 2: 31.3 %, 3: 20.9 % | 2: 31.3 %, 3: 57.5 %, 4: 10.4 % | 0.8 % / 0.7 % | 6 / 7 | **20.7 %** (21.0 %) |

Node 5 led nothing at five members: it votes in neither group, so it
cannot lead one. A step-down can return the lead to the same node, so
changes are fewer than events.

**Reading:** elections alone neither co-locate nor spread the two
leaders. At three members both groups share all three voters, and the
leaders sat on one node 42 % of the led time, near the one-in-three that
independent elections would give. From four members the groups share
only nodes 2 and 3, and co-location fell to about 21 %. Placement
follows which voter wins each election. One run per size on loopback
gives the shape, not a rate to quote: the order of events and
election timing decide it. The real-host measurement under A11 load is
the parent's.

Raw: the three `placement-m{3,4,5}.json` (every sample and event) and
`.txt` (tables), the driver `place-run.sh` and its log, in the raw
directory below.

## Limitations and remaining gates

  build hosts).
- The leader placement figures are loopback runs on one host, TCP, with no
  client load. The real-host measurement under A11 load is the parent's.
