## Follow-up: observed cut collisions and safe design constraints

AMD reports instrumented d485afbf original-budget direct seven/jobs16 failure: 71+75 transaction revision mismatches, 18 exhausted capture BUSY per publisher, one authority BUSY at 402 ms, successful build/checkpoint maxima 10071/8950 ms, no logged pending-record refusal and publication writer-lock wait maximum zero. These are AMD-reported observations, not a new Mac run. Synchronous instrumentation perturbs timings; early lag-update lock wait was not measured. The collision mechanism is observed, but exclusive causality and quiet acceptance remain unproved.

Moving the existing pair proof before pinning and eliminating its post-capture revision comparison is not safe. Both group admissions have bounded commit targets; new work can commit/apply between admission and pin. A distributed rename may have its first participant fully resolved locally (markers removed, first namespace effect visible), while a second participant's already committed PREP/resolve has not yet applied locally. The pin can therefore contain one side with no visible pending marker for the absent side. Fresh post-pin group proofs advance that participant; the captured/live revision mismatch rejects the mixed image. Keeping a post-proof revision fence and adding a pre-proof may change performance but does not remove the rejecting window or guarantee progress. Holding h->mu through a fresh proof is also unsafe: the proof depends on pump/apply paths acquiring that mutex.

The smallest ordering negative control should run the actual host capture helper with an explicit two-participant fixture: pin records first side resolved/second side absent, fresh post-pin pair barrier delivers the second PREP/resolve and advances the transaction revision, and the candidate is rejected/released. Mutants that skip the comparison or substitute a pre-pin-only proof must incorrectly accept the mixed image. Companion controls: ordinary single-entry writes do not invalidate; unrelated transaction changes still safely refuse under current contract; import generation changes, term/authority refusal, I/O failure and inherited expiry refuse; final stable retry succeeds and releases every failed pin. Use real LSM marker/revision tests in addition to the host mock; the mock alone is not a durable coherent-cut proof.

A smaller potential cause discriminator is transaction-revision advancement counts by marker kind and PUT/DEL, between pin and proof completion, correlated with applied-index progress. Current global fence increments for INTENT/GUARD/REDUCE/DECISION. Do not filter coordinator decisions or completed transaction cleanup without proving participant completion/retirement coverage. A truly less conservative cut requires durable participant/decision dependency coverage at the captured apply vector, or a validated boundary at which all transaction effects are represented; a simple ordering edit is insufficient. This is larger correctness work rather than a proven minimal patch.

Fresh root authority is independently capable of BUSY even with a completed catalog: query_auth_dir explicitly returns BUSY when a live root is absent from the catalog, when generation/parent differs, or when its binding no longer matches. Fresh transactional inode/dentry reads can also refuse. Split these reason codes while preserving authorization and ancestry checks. For missing/changed root, correlate current root identity with the next published generation and exact pending marker/capture phase. A root-triggered refresh could avoid scheduler idle time but cannot cure a collided cut or an 8–10-second build by itself; serving a fresh inode with stale subtree data would be incorrect. No safe root fallback or capture reorder is justified yet.
