### Device/cache hypothesis: identified, still unproven

The October 11 read-only sysfs snapshot identifies Samsung SSD 970 EVO 1TB,
firmware `2B2QEXE7`, NVMe queue `write back` and scheduler `none`; SATA identifies
HP `MK0800GEYKE`, firmware `HPG1`, queue `write through` and scheduler
`mq-deadline`. NVMe core's configured maximum power-state latency is 100,000 us;
this does not identify which state the drive actually entered. No setting changed.

Linux documents distinct volatile-cache flush/FUA handling, including completing
empty preflush requests in the block layer for devices reported without a volatile
write cache. The SATA/NVMe difference is consistent with the measured absence of
separately issued SATA flush requests, but the sysfs flag does not prove the
physical SATA drive's power-loss protection or isolate a firmware cause.
[Kernel cache-control contract](https://docs.kernel.org/block/writeback_cache_control.html).

Samsung documents TurboWrite buffering and different performance outside that
region; its published rates are not per-fsync latency guarantees.
[970 EVO datasheet](https://download.semiconductor.samsung.com/resources/data-sheet/Samsung-NVMe-SSD-970-EVO-Data-Sheet_Rev.1.0.pdf).
The three timed cells issue approximately 4.011, 18.805 and 4.056 GiB of host
NVMe writes respectively. Prior preparation/writes, cache occupancy and internal
flash writes are not measured by those totals. They do not demonstrate cache
exhaustion. Keep cache/firmware, filesystem and block-queue attribution open;
preserve durability barriers rather than treating advertised bandwidth as the
ceiling for this workload.

### Client batching feedback defect: fixed, full Linux acceptance pending

The late client trace shows APPEND_RESERVE (type 71) and REPORT (type 67)
each taking roughly 1.1–1.2 s, with publication after nearly every write.
`append_publish_burst` reset its one-second age clock before flushing. Once
that flush exceeds a second, the next write immediately times out the new
burst's age even though its first bytes have not yet been accepted. This is
a software amplification of persistence latency, not a hardware ceiling.

`f52cd2dd` starts the new age window after successful publication, retains the
32-write bound and existing 100 ms peer handoff, and forces the next admission
to retry a failed publication instead of silently starting a fresh burst.
No RPC deadline or durability barrier changes. The regression executes the
actual admission function with controlled time: a 1.2 s publication must allow
a new 32-write burst; a 1.5 s age-triggered publication must not immediately
expire its successor; failed drains must retry until successful. It passes
with the fix and fails when the old age-reset behavior is restored. The test
is included in `make test`. This code is committed but not installed or
performance-accepted; full Linux/native, direct/buffered and exact append
validation must follow the same-engine W97 placement control.
