## What changed since the early measurement

- **Budget:** a per-group token bucket of files. Tokens accrue at 5,000
  files/s, or at 200 files/s while the cluster's foreground commit rate
  (commits of every hosted group not proposed by the packer) exceeds 100/s.
  A segment is built only when the bucket holds its file count, so
  segments stay full. The rate is cluster-wide because both groups share the
  same nodes: a first per-group version stayed at −19% for one writer whose
  files landed in the other group.
- **Compaction:** segments with live share below 50%, at least 32 KiB dead,
  or under 8 KiB live (merged with other small ones) are read back (any two
  fragments), their live records still referenced by their files are copied
  into cold segments, and the switch releases the old records; emptied
  segments retire through the journal.
- **Backfill:** the leader walks its group's inode shards (64 per pass),
  proposes PACK_INDEX (MD 78) for regular files of at most 16 KiB with a
  per-file base, and keeps a per-group cursor until done.

## Compaction and backfill checks

- **Compaction** (node9901 cluster, packer build, minimum age 2 s): 4,000
  files from four workers packed into 10 segments (692,662 B per node);
  3,750 deleted at random, 250 kept (seed 7); 60 s later 3 segments
  (53,507 B). Four compaction batches moved 416 records for 250 survivors:
  small segments get merged more than once before they settle (a follow-up
  to tighten).
- **Backfill:** base build writes 3,000 files (4 workers) and stops; the
  packer build starts on the same stores with minimum age 2 s; within 100 s
  every file is indexed (`backfill group=2 complete, 3000 files indexed`) and
  packed (11 segments, 0 per-file fragments); `mdraft/kv/MANIFEST` is
  KVLSM2; the namespace lists 3,000 files.

## Limitations

- Packed content is not read back: the client read path is P3.
- One synthetic workload; node9901 only; three runs per configuration.
- The candidate index costs one KV row per small file until it is packed.
- Compaction re-merges small segments more than needed.
